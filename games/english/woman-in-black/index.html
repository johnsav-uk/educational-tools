Reading help
The first step

Choose your light

Every room works at three levels. Change your light whenever you like. A brighter light means harder questions and less help.

The second step

Five rooms, five tasks

Each room revises one thing. Finish a room to light its window. Light all five to make it back across the causeway.

Word bank

Passages are retold in our own words, with the chapter they come from, so they match any edition. Find the real lines in your copy and quote them in your own answers.

All five windows are lit

You made it back across the causeway

The tide is turning behind you. You have revised the gothic setting, characters and plot of the novel, the key words to describe them, and written your own gothic opening. Try every room again with a brighter light.

For teachers

One resource, three levels. Each room asks about the same part of the novel at every level, so a mixed class can work on one room together and share answers. Pupils can change level at any time; nothing is locked.

Candle: KS2, working towardsShort, simple retellings shown above the fuller version. Two choices at a time. The word bank has pictures and everyday definitions. Read aloud speaks any text that is tapped.
Lamp: KS3, on trackFull retellings, four choices, then a second question on the writer's effect. Sentence builder gives whole sentences labelled with their techniques.
Lantern: KS3, working beyondTyped answers and paragraphs with what / how / why sentence starters, compared against a model. Matching uses examples from the novel instead of definitions. The word bank is folded away.

Rooms: the Causeway (spot the technique), the Nursery (sort the gothic features), the Graveyard (key words), Mrs Drablow's papers (plot order and foreshadowing) and Write by candlelight (a gothic opening). Progress is kept only on the pupil's own device. "No fog or jumps" removes the fog to rub away, the bats and drifting mist, the flickering flames and the figure that appears in the graveyard when a room is finished. "Easier reading" sets every passage on plain cream paper in the site's clear font.
