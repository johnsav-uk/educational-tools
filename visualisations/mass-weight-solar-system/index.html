Key idea

Mass and weight are not the same thing

Mass stays the same everywhere, but weight changes depending on gravity!

m

Mass

kilograms (kg)
  • The amount of matter (stuff) in an object.
  • Measured with a balance.
  • Stays the same wherever you go in the Universe.
  • It is not a force.
W

Weight

newtons (N)
  • The force of gravity pulling on an object's mass.
  • Measured with a newtonmeter (spring balance).
  • Changes when the gravitational field strength changes.
  • It is a force, so it acts in a direction: towards the centre of the planet.

Watch out: in everyday life people say “I weigh 45 kg”. In science that number is your mass. Your weight on Earth is about 441 N.

The equation

Working out weight: W = m × g

W=m×g
W
weight, in newtons (N)
m
mass, in kilograms (kg)
g
gravitational field strength, in newtons per kilogram (N/kg)

On Earth g = 9.8 N/kg: every kilogram is pulled down with a force of 9.8 newtons. Many KS3 questions round this to 10 N/kg, so read the question.

      Are mass and weight the same thing?

      An astronaut steps on bathroom scales on Earth. They read 80 kg.

      She flies to the Moon and stands on the same scales.

      1. A The scales still read 80 kg.
      2. B The scales read less than 80 kg.
      3. C The scales read more than 80 kg.

      Think, pair, share: which is it, and has anything about the astronaut really changed?

      B. The scales read about 13 kg. Scales really measure the push of your weight, then divide by Earth's 9.8 N/kg.

      But she is not made of less stuff. Her mass is still 80 kg. It's her weight that dropped, from 784 N to 128 N, because the Moon's gravity is weaker.
