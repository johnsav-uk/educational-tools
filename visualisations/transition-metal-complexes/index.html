Complex ions: ligands, shapes and isomerism: mark scheme

Teacher copy · AQA A-level Chemistry 3.2.5.1, 3.2.5.2, 3.2.5.3
Savage Science Tools

1.1 [1]

  • a molecule or ion that forms a co-ordinate bond with a transition metal (atom or ion) by donating a pair of electrons

Do not accept “donates electrons” without “pair” / “lone pair”.

1.2 [1]

  • the number of co-ordinate bonds to the central metal atom or ion

Do not accept “the number of ligands” (a bidentate ligand forms two bonds).

1.3 [1]

  • each ligand forms two co-ordinate bonds / donates two lone pairs (one from each N atom)

1.4 [2]

  • 1s2 2s2 2p6 3s2 3p6 3d9 / [Ar] 3d9 (1)
  • (it forms an ion with) an incomplete d sub-level (1)

Do not accept 4s1 3d8 or any configuration with 4s electrons.

2.1 [3]

  • [CuCl4]2− (1)
  • yellow / yellow-green (1)
  • tetrahedral (1)

The charge must be correct for the formula mark. Allow “green” only if “yellow” is also given.

2.2 [1]

  • Cl− ligands are larger than H2O, so only four fit round the Cu2+

2.3 [2]

  • [Cu(H2O)6]2+ + 4NH3 → [Cu(NH3)4(H2O)2]2+ + 4H2O
  • correct formula of the complex (1); balanced (1)

2.4 [1]

  • NH3 and H2O are similar in size (and both uncharged)

3.1 [3]

  • similar bonds (six co-ordinate bonds) are broken and made, so ΔH is about zero (1)
  • the number of particles increases from 4 to 7, so entropy increases / ΔS is positive (1)
  • so ΔG (= ΔH − TΔS) is negative (1)

Do not accept “more bonds are formed”.

3.2 [2]

  • [Cu(H2O)6]2+ + EDTA4− → [Cu(EDTA)]2− + 6H2O (1)
  • 2 particles become 7 (1)

3.3 [2]

  • CO forms a (stronger) co-ordinate bond with the Fe2+ / iron(II) in haemoglobin, replacing the oxygen (1)
  • so oxygen cannot be carried in the blood (1)

4.1 [2]

  • [H3N→Ag←NH3]+ drawn linear, with the co-ordinate bonds from N (1)
  • 180° (1)

4.2 [2]

  • square planar with the two Cl next to each other / at 90° (1)
  • cis–trans isomerism / E–Z / geometric isomerism (1)

4.3 [1]

  • 90°

4.4 [3]

  • 3D octahedral structure with three en, each bonded at two adjacent positions (1)
  • second structure drawn as the mirror image of the first (1)
  • 2+ charge shown on both (1)

Each en must span adjacent (cis) positions: an en drawn across opposite positions scores 0 for the first mark.

4.5 [2]

  • the two Cl− can be next to each other (90°, cis) or opposite each other (180°, trans) (1)
  • each isomer has a plane of symmetry / its mirror image is superimposable / it has only monodentate ligands (1)

4.6 [3]

  • a trans isomer, with the two Cl opposite each other (1)
  • a cis isomer, with the two Cl next to each other (1)
  • the cis isomer has a non-superimposable mirror image, so it exists as two optical isomers / the trans isomer has a plane of symmetry so it does not (1)

Coloured ions, colorimetry and metal-aqua ions: mark scheme

Teacher copy · AQA A-level Chemistry 3.2.5.4, 3.2.6
Savage Science Tools

1.1 [3]

  • d electrons absorb (visible) light and move from the ground state to an excited state (1)
  • light of a particular frequency / wavelength is absorbed, whose energy matches ΔE (red / orange light) (1)
  • the remaining frequencies are transmitted / reflected, and they look blue (1)

Do not accept “electrons fall back and give out (blue) light”: that is emission, which loses the first mark.

1.2 [2]

  • ΔE = hc/λ = (6.63 × 10−34 × 3.00 × 108) ÷ (600 × 10−9) (1)
  • = 3.32 × 10−19 J (1)

Wavelength not converted to metres scores 0. Allow 3.3 × 10−19.

1.3 [1]

  • ν = c/λ = 3.00 × 108 ÷ 600 × 10−9 = 5.00 × 1014 Hz

Allow ν = ΔE/h. Allow s−1.

1.4 [1]

  • 3.32 × 10−19 × 6.022 × 1023 ÷ 1000 = 200 kJ mol−1

Allow 199–200. Allow consequential marking from 1.2.

1.5 [2]

  • a different ligand (NH3) changes ΔE / the energy gap between the d orbitals (1)
  • so a different frequency / wavelength of light is absorbed (1)

1.6 [2]

  • Zn2+ has a full d sub-level / 3d10 (1)
  • no d electron can move to a higher energy level, so no visible light is absorbed (1)

2.1 [2]

  • red (allow orange) (1)
  • it is the complementary colour of blue / it is the colour the solution absorbs most (1)

2.2 [3]

  • all five points plotted correctly, to within half a small square (1)
  • straight line of best fit through the origin (1)
  • 0.35 mol dm−3 (allow 0.34–0.36) (1)

2.3 [1]

  • absorbance is (directly) proportional to concentration

2.4 [1]

  • the ammonia complex is a deeper / more intense colour, so it gives a larger absorbance for the same concentration

3.1 [6]

  • green precipitate (1)
  • blue precipitate, then (dissolves in excess to give) a deep blue solution (1)
  • brown precipitate and effervescence / bubbles (1)
  • white precipitate, which dissolves (in excess) to give a colourless solution (1)
  • blue-green precipitate (1)
  • brown precipitate (1)

Allow “gas given off” for effervescence; do not accept “CO2 given off” alone, as that is not an observation. For [Fe(H2O)6]2+, allow “green precipitate that darkens / turns brown on standing”.

3.2 [2]

  • 2[Fe(H2O)6]3+ + 3CO32− → 2[Fe(H2O)3(OH)3] + 3CO2 + 3H2O
  • correct species (1); balanced (1)

Do not accept Fe2(CO3)3 as a product.

3.3 [3]

  • Fe3+ is smaller and more highly charged / has a higher charge density (1)
  • it polarises / weakens the O–H bonds in the water ligands more (1)
  • so H+ ions are released more readily / the equilibrium [Fe(H2O)6]3+ ⇌ [Fe(H2O)5(OH)]2+ + H+ lies further to the right (1)

3.4 [2]

  • [Al(H2O)3(OH)3] + 3H+ → [Al(H2O)6]3+ (1)
  • [Al(H2O)3(OH)3] + OH− → [Al(H2O)2(OH)4]− + H2O (1)

Allow [Al(OH)4]− with 3H2O. Allow H3O+ for H+ with the water balanced.
