    • 1 and 3 (23.85 and 23.95)

    1.7 [2]

    • (23.85 + 23.95) ÷ 2 (1)
    • = 23.90 (cm³) (1)

    An answer using all four titres (24.15) or all three precise titres (24.00) scores 0.

    2.1 [3]

    • moles HCl = 0.100 × 23.90 ÷ 1000 = 2.39 × 10−3 (1)
    • moles NaOH = 2.39 × 10−3 (1:1 ratio) (1)
    • concentration = 2.39 × 10−3 ÷ (25.0 ÷ 1000) = 0.0956 mol/dm³ (1)

    1.1 [2]

    • moles = 0.100 × 250.0 ÷ 1000 = 0.0250 (1)
    • mass = 0.0250 × 106.0 = 2.65 g (1)

    1.2 [5]

    • dissolve in (less than 250 cm³ of) deionised / distilled water, stirring with a glass rod (1)
    • transfer to a (250 cm³) volumetric flask using a funnel (1)
    • rinse the beaker, rod and funnel with deionised water and add the washings to the flask (1)
    • make up to the mark with deionised water, adding it drop by drop near the mark (1)
    • bottom of the meniscus on the mark, viewed at eye level; stopper and invert several times to mix (1)

    Do not accept “tap water”. Mixing must be by inverting the stoppered flask, not shaking an open one.

    1.3 [2]

    • mass = 11.37 − 8.71 = 2.66 g; uncertainty = 2 × 0.005 = 0.01 g (1)
    • 0.01 ÷ 2.66 × 100 = 0.38 % (1)

    0.19 % (one reading only) scores 1.

    1.4 [2]

    • the concentration is lower than calculated (1)
    • because some sodium carbonate (solution) is left in the beaker and not transferred to the flask (1)

    1.5 [3]

    • c(Na2CO3) = 2.66 ÷ 106.0 ÷ 0.2500 = 0.1004 mol dm−3; moles in 25.0 cm³ = 2.509 × 10−3 (1)
    • moles HCl = 2 × 2.509 × 10−3 = 5.019 × 10−3 (1)
    • concentration = 5.019 × 10−3 ÷ 0.02435 = 0.206 mol dm−3 (1)

    0.103 (ratio not used) scores 2. Allow ECF.

    2.1 [2]

    • read the pH at half the equivalence volume (12.5 cm³), where [CH3COOH] = [CH3COO−] so pH = pKa (1)
    • pKa = 4.8 (allow 4.7 – 4.8) (1)

    2.2 [2]

    • phenolphthalein (1)
    • its range (8.3 – 10.0) lies within the steep / vertical part of the curve (around pH 7 – 11) (1)

    Do not accept “the equivalence point is at pH 7”.

    2.3 [1]

    • its range (3.1 – 4.4) is below the steep part / in the buffer region, so it changes colour (gradually) well before the equivalence point

    3.1 [1]

    • MnO4− + 8H+ + 5Fe2+ → Mn2+ + 4H2O + 5Fe3+

    Ignore state symbols. Electrons must not appear.

    3.2 [4]

    • moles MnO4− = 0.0100 × 21.60 ÷ 1000 = 2.160 × 10−4 (1)
    • moles Fe2+ in 25.0 cm³ = 5 × 2.160 × 10−4 = 1.080 × 10−3 (1)
    • moles in 250.0 cm³ = 1.080 × 10−2 (1)
    • mass = 1.080 × 10−2 × 55.8 = 0.603 g (1)

    0.0603 g (scaling to 250 cm³ missed) scores 3. 0.121 g (1:1 ratio) scores 2. Allow ECF.

    3.3 [1]

    • (colourless / pale yellow) to (pale) pink, which stays

    Do not accept purple to colourless; do not accept “purple” for the endpoint.

    3.4 [2]

    • chloride ions are oxidised (to chlorine) by manganate(VII) (1)
    • so more manganate(VII) is used / the titre is too big (so the calculated amount of iron is too high) (1)

    4.1 [3]

    • at first the reaction is slow because it is between two negative ions, which repel / the activation energy is high (1)
    • Mn2+ ions formed in the reaction catalyse it: autocatalysis (1)
    • Mn2+ provides a route with a lower activation energy (e.g. 4Mn2+ + MnO4− + 8H+ → 5Mn3+ + 4H2O, then 2Mn3+ + C2O42− → 2Mn2+ + 2CO2), so the reaction speeds up as more Mn2+ forms (1)

    4.2 [3]

    • moles MnO4− = 0.0200 × 23.20 ÷ 1000 = 4.640 × 10−4 (1)
    • moles C2O42− = 4.640 × 10−4 × 5 ÷ 2 = 1.160 × 10−3 (1)
    • concentration = 1.160 × 10−3 ÷ 0.0250 = 0.0464 mol dm−3 (1)

    0.0186 (1 : 1 ratio used) or 7.42 × 10−3 (ratio inverted) scores 2.

    5.1 [1]

    • 2 × 0.05 = 0.10; 0.10 ÷ 21.60 × 100 = 0.46 %

    0.23 % (one reading only) scores 0.

    5.2 [1]

    • make the titre bigger: use a larger portion in the flask, a more concentrated solution in the flask, or a more dilute solution in the burette

    Do not accept “repeat the titration” or “use a more precise burette”.
