Every nucleus has the same chance of decaying each second. You can't say which one goes next.

Half-life

The time for the number of undecayed nuclei, or the activity, to fall to half.

Reading it off the graph

Nuclear equation rules

The top numbers (mass numbers) balance on each side, and so do the bottom numbers (atomic numbers).

α: 22688Ra → 22286Rn + 42He

β: 146C → 147N + 0−1e

Alpha and beta decay change the element (the atomic number changes). Gamma emission changes neither number. Neutron emission (10n) lowers the mass number by 1 and leaves the element the same.

Half-life and activity

Activity is the rate at which a source of unstable nuclei decays, measured in becquerels (Bq). 1 Bq = 1 decay per second.

Count rate is the number of decays recorded each second by a detector such as a Geiger-Müller tube.

Half-life is the time it takes for the number of nuclei of the isotope in a sample to halve, or the time for the count rate (or activity) to fall to half its initial level.

Radioactive decay is random: you can't predict which nucleus will decay next, or when.

HT After n half-lives, the fraction left is 1 / 2n. After 3 half-lives, 1/8 is left, a net decline of 7/8.

Measuring it in the lab

Measure the background count first, with no source, and subtract it from every reading.

Count for a longer time, or repeat and average, because the readings fluctuate at random.

Keep the distance fixed when you test absorbers: count rate also falls as the detector moves away.

Contamination and irradiation

Contamination is unwanted radioactive atoms getting onto or into other materials. Irradiation is exposing an object to nuclear radiation. An irradiated object does not become radioactive.

Alpha is the most dangerous inside the body (strongly ionising) and the least dangerous outside it, because skin stops it.

Alpha, α

A helium nucleus: 42He

  • 2 protons and 2 neutrons, charge +2
  • Strongly ionising
  • Range in air: about 5 cm
  • Stopped by a sheet of paper, or skin
Beta, β

A fast electron: 0−1e

  • A neutron turns into a proton and the electron is thrown out
  • Moderately ionising
  • Range in air: up to about 1 m
  • Stopped by a few mm of aluminium
Gamma, γ

An electromagnetic wave

  • Comes from the nucleus, charge 0
  • Weakly ionising
  • Travels a long way in air
  • Lead or concrete only reduces it
