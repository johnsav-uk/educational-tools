  • zinc is more reactive than copper (1)

1.2 [2]

  • the voltage depends on the difference in reactivity / magnesium and zinc are closer together in reactivity than magnesium and copper (1)

1.3 [1]

  • the metals are the same, so there is no difference in reactivity

1.4 [1]

  • any one from: the electrolyte / its concentration; the temperature; the depth the metals are dipped in / the distance between them; the same voltmeter

Do not accept “the metals” (this is the independent variable).

1.5 [1]

  • (0.74 + 0.77 + 0.75) ÷ 3 = 0.75 (V)

2.1 [1]

  • 4.5 V

2.2 [1]

  • the chemical reactions stop when one of the reactants has been used up

Do not accept “it runs out of electrons / charge / energy”.

2.3 [1]

  • an external electrical current is supplied, which reverses the chemical reactions

3.1 [2]

  • 2H2 + O2 → 2H2O: correct formulae (1); balanced (1)

Allow multiples, e.g. H2 + ½O2 → H2O.

3.2 [2]

  • advantage, any one: the only product is water; refuelled quickly; does not need recharging from the mains; does not lose capacity with repeated recharging (1)
  • disadvantage, any one: hydrogen is a flammable gas / difficult to store; hydrogen is often made from fossil fuels / its production needs energy; few refuelling stations; fuel cells are expensive (1)

3.3 [2]

  • negative electrode: 2H2 → 4H+ + 4e− (1)
  • positive electrode: O2 + 4H+ + 4e− → 2H2O (1)

Allow multiples (e.g. H2 → 2H+ + 2e−). Alkaline equations score only if the student states an alkaline electrolyte; the question says acidic.

4.1 [2]

  • when molten the ions are free to move (and carry the charge) (1)

Do not accept “electrons move”: 0 marks for the reference to electrons.

4.2 [2]

Do not accept “bromide” for bromine.

4.3 [2]

  • sodium is more reactive than hydrogen (1)

4.4 [2]

Do not accept “sulfur” or “sulfur dioxide” at the anode.

Electrode potentials and electrochemical cells: mark scheme

Teacher copy · AQA A-level Chemistry 3.1.11 · Required practical 8
Savage Science Tools

1.1 [1]

  • Zn(s) | Zn2+(aq) || Cu2+(aq) | Cu(s)

Ignore state symbols. Do not accept a single line for the salt bridge. By convention the negative electrode (zinc, oxidation) is written on the left; the sides swapped scores only if E°cell in 1.2 is then given as −1.10 V for the cell as written.

1.2 [1]

  • (+0.34) − (−0.76) = +1.10 V

1.3 [2]

  • zinc is the negative electrode (1)
  • electrons flow (through the wire) from zinc to copper (1)

Do not accept electrons flowing through the salt bridge or the solution.

1.4 [3]

  • allows ions to move (between the half-cells) / completes the circuit (1)
  • maintains electrical neutrality / balances the charge in each half-cell (1)
  • chloride ions would react with silver ions to form a precipitate of silver chloride (so the concentration of Ag+ would change) (1)

Do not accept “allows electrons to flow”.

1.5 [1]

  • so that (almost) no current flows, so the measured potential difference is the EMF / the concentrations do not change

2.1 [1]

  • Zn + 2Ag+ → Zn2+ + 2Ag

Electrons must not appear. Ignore state symbols.

2.2 [2]

  • electrode potentials must not be multiplied (by the coefficient in the balanced equation) (1)
  • (+0.80) − (−0.76) = +1.56 V (1)

3.1 [3]

  • E°cell = 0.77 − 0.54 = +0.23 V (1)
  • positive (so the reaction is feasible) / E°(Fe3+/Fe2+) is more positive than E°(I2/I−) (1)
  • 2Fe3+ + 2I− → 2Fe2+ + I2 (1)

3.2 [2]

  • E°cell for Cu2+ + 2Fe2+ → Cu + 2Fe3+ = 0.34 − 0.77 = −0.43 V (1)
  • negative, so not feasible / E°(Cu2+/Cu) is less positive than E°(Fe3+/Fe2+), so Cu2+ is the weaker oxidising agent (1)

3.3 [1]

  • the activation energy is high / the reaction is very slow (E° tells you nothing about the rate); or the conditions are not standard

4.1 [3]

  • hydrogen gas bubbled over a platinum electrode (1)
  • dipping into a solution of H+ ions at 1.00 mol dm−3 (e.g. 1.00 mol dm−3 HCl) (1)
  • at 298 K and 100 kPa (1)

Allow 1 bar; allow platinum black; allow 0.5 mol dm−3 H2SO4. Do not accept 1 atm or 101 kPa. Do not accept 1 mol dm−3 H2SO4.

4.2 [2]

  • E°cell = E°right − E°left = −0.44 − 0.00 = −0.44 V (1)
  • negative, so the reaction as written (H2 reducing Fe2+) is not feasible; the reverse is: iron is oxidised and the iron electrode is the negative electrode (1)

5.1 [6]

One mark for each point, up to 6:

  • clean the copper and silver electrodes with emery paper
  • copper in 1.00 mol dm−3 copper(II) sulfate solution; silver in 1.00 mol dm−3 silver nitrate solution, in separate beakers
  • salt bridge: filter paper soaked in saturated potassium nitrate solution dipping into both solutions
  • connect the electrodes to a high-resistance voltmeter
  • record the reading (and its sign / which electrode is positive) once it is steady
  • at 298 K / a controlled temperature; repeat with fresh solutions and filter paper and take a mean

A salt bridge of potassium chloride loses that mark (reacts with Ag+).

5.2 [2]

  • E°cell = 0.80 − 0.34 = +0.46 V (1)
  • any two reasons for (1): concentrations not exactly 1.00 mol dm−3; temperature not 298 K; oxide layer / impurities on the electrodes; a small current flows through the voltmeter

Do not accept “electrons lost in the salt bridge” or “electrodes too small”.

5.3 [3]

  • the EMF increases (1)
  • a lower [Cu2+] moves the equilibrium Cu2+ + 2e− ⇌ Cu to the left (1)
  • so the copper electrode potential becomes less positive / more negative; the copper electrode is the negative electrode in this cell, so the difference between the electrode potentials increases (1)
